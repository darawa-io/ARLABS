Concept

Point Charge & Force on It

A point charge is a charged object treated as having negligible mass and size — it takes up virtually no space, so we can talk about "the force at that point" without worrying about its shape.

The center sphere is our point charge, q₀. It sits inside the field of other point charges. Each one pushes or pulls on q₀ along the line joining them — same sign repels, opposite sign attracts.

The net force is the vector sum of all of these individual forces. If q₀ is free to move, it accelerates in the direction of the net force — press "Release q₀" to watch it happen. Use the buttons below to hide the resultant arrow, or switch between the 2D (3 sources) and 1D (2 sources) setups.

Coulomb's Law

The electric force between two stationary point charges is:

F = (1 / 4πε₀) · q₁q₂ / r²

Three properties to notice as you drag the sliders:

• The force acts along the line joining the two charges.

• It is inversely proportional to r² — double the separation and the force drops to a quarter.

• It is proportional to the product of the magnitudes |q₁||q₂|.

Same sign → repulsive. Opposite sign → attractive.

Superposition Principle

When several point charges act on one charge at once, the net force is the vector sum of every individual force:

ΣF = F₁ + F₂ + ⋯

Coulomb's law is applied to each pair separately, then the resulting vectors are added head-to-tail (or by components) — never just their magnitudes.

Two source charges surround a test charge qₜ. Each produces its own force (amber, violet); the tip-to-tail diagram on the right shows how they combine into the resultant (green). Use the ◀▶▲▼ pads to drag each source around the plane — the top-view grid above shows exact (x, z) coordinates.

1D case Two charges sit on a line. Slide the test position and watch the net force — electrostatic equilibrium is the point where the forces from both charges exactly cancel, so the net force is zero.

If the charges have opposite signs, that point lies outside the pair, beyond the weaker charge. If they have the same sign, it lies between them.
