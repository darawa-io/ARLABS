One family, seven regions

Shared by every EM wave

✓ Transverse waves — oscillations in the electric and magnetic field are at right angles to the direction of travel.
✓ Transfer energy as radiation, from a source to an absorber.
✓ Can travel through a vacuum, such as the vacuum of space.
✓ All travel at the same speed through a vacuum (or air): 3 × 10⁸ m/s.
✓ Together they form one continuous spectrum, split into 7 regions by wavelength/frequency.
Within visible light, red has the lowest frequency and violet the highest — that ordering (red→violet) continues the pattern of the whole spectrum (low freq→high freq).

Communication systems rely on EM radiation

• Mobile phones & wireless internet use microwaves — they penetrate some walls and only need a short aerial.
• Bluetooth uses radio waves — they pass through walls, though the signal weakens doing so.
• Optical fibres use visible light or infrared — glass is transparent to them, and they can carry very high data rates.
• Satellite communication is mainly by microwaves: low-orbit satellites for some satellite phones; geostationary satellites for some satellite phones and direct broadcast TV.
Application · Communicating Using Satellites

Two ways to bounce a signal off space

Communication with artificial satellites is mainly by microwaves. A geostationary satellite stays above one point on Earth's surface and covers a large area; low Earth orbit satellites move around the Earth and cover a much smaller area, so several are needed to relay a signal on. Other Idea · Digital and Analogue Signals

Two ways to carry the same information

A sound, image or any other information can be carried as an analogue signal (continuous in value and time) or a digital signal (discrete — on/off, represented by binary numbers).

0%

Definitions

Analogue: a signal that is continuous in both value and time. All naturally-occurring signals are analogue.

Digital: a signal that is discrete in both value and time — either on or off — represented using binary numbers.

Why digital wins for data

✓ Higher rate of transmission — more data, faster and more accurately.
✓ Longer range — a digital signal can be regenerated accurately, even after noise has been picked up.
✓ Digital signals over optical fibre carry more data than a copper cable.

Try the noise slider: an analogue wave gets permanently distorted, but a digital signal can still be read correctly and regenerated cleanly — until the noise is severe enough to flip a bit.
